What Do We Consider a Correction?

Correction vs Update: What Is the Difference?

Not every content change means the original article contained an error.

Change Type Meaning Example
Correction Previously published information was materially inaccurate. A page states RM10 when the verified offer was RM5.
Update The information was accurate but later changed. A promotion previously offered RM10 but later changed to RM5.
Clarification The underlying information may be correct but needs clearer explanation. Explaining that promotional credit is not automatically withdrawable cash.
Editorial Improvement The information remains accurate but presentation or usability is improved. Adding a comparison table or clearer internal navigation.

Our 6-Step Correction Process

1. Identify the Disputed Information

We first identify the exact claim, page, table, link or statement that may be inaccurate.

2. Review the Original Context

We examine how the information is presented and whether the surrounding context changes its meaning.

3. Check Current Sources

Where possible, we prioritise primary or authoritative sources relevant to the disputed claim.

Depending on the issue, this may include official platform pages, official promotion terms, provider information, campaign pages or other directly relevant documentation.

4. Determine Whether a Change Is Required

We compare the published claim with the available evidence and determine whether the page requires a correction, update, clarification or no change.

5. Correct the Content

When the evidence supports a correction, we update the affected statement and surrounding information where necessary so that the page remains understandable.

6. Check Related Pages

If the same claim may appear elsewhere, we may review related FreeKreditRM10 pages to reduce the risk of inconsistent information across the website.

What Is a Material Correction?

A material correction changes information that could significantly affect a reader's understanding or decision.

Examples can include:

  • changing whether a deposit is required;
  • correcting the advertised promotional amount;
  • correcting wagering requirements;
  • correcting withdrawal restrictions;
  • correcting the responsible platform or provider;
  • correcting eligibility requirements;
  • correcting an active promotion that has actually expired; or
  • removing an unsupported guarantee.

What Changes Are Not Usually Material Corrections?

Minor editorial changes generally do not alter the underlying meaning of the page.

Examples include:

  • fixing spelling or grammar;
  • improving sentence structure;
  • updating formatting;
  • repairing a broken internal link;
  • improving accessibility;
  • adding clearer headings; or
  • improving navigation without changing the factual claim.

How We Handle Updated Dates

A page should not appear newly reviewed merely because of an automated WordPress date change.

Where a visible "updated" or "reviewed" date is used, it should ideally correspond to a meaningful review or material content update.

Minor formatting changes alone should not be used to create a misleading impression that the underlying information was fully reverified.
