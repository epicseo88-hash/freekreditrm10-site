What Is Responsible Gaming?

Responsible gaming means treating gambling as entertainment rather than income, setting affordable financial and time limits before playing, understanding the rules of promotions and stopping when gambling begins to negatively affect your finances, work, relationships or wellbeing.

A bonus or promotional balance should never be treated as guaranteed profit or as a reason to spend more than you originally planned.

7 Responsible Gaming Rules

1. Set a Spending Limit Before Playing

Decide how much money you can comfortably treat as entertainment expenditure before you begin.

Your gambling budget should never include money required for food, rent, bills, education, debt payments, family expenses or savings.

2. Set a Time Limit

Decide when your session will end before you begin playing.

Take regular breaks. Do not extend a session because you believe a win is about to happen.

3. Never Chase Losses

Chasing losses means continuing to deposit or gamble because you are trying to recover money already lost.

A previous loss does not make a future win more likely.

4. Understand the Promotion Before Claiming It

A promotion can have conditions affecting how promotional balances and resulting winnings are used.

Check eligibility, deposit requirements, wagering, eligible games, expiry and withdrawal conditions.

5. Do Not Gamble With Borrowed Money

Borrowing money to continue gambling increases financial risk and can turn entertainment spending into debt.

6. Do Not Treat Gambling as Income

Gambling outcomes are uncertain. A free-credit promotion does not transform gambling into a predictable income source.

7. Know When to Stop

Stop when you reach your predetermined money or time limit.

If gambling begins affecting your finances, relationships, work, study or emotional wellbeing, stopping is more important than continuing to pursue a bonus or win.

Warning Signs That Gambling May Be Becoming a Problem

Gambling behaviour can become harmful gradually. Recognising changes early can make it easier to regain control.

  • Spending more money than you originally planned.
  • Repeatedly increasing deposits after losses.
  • Borrowing money to continue gambling.
  • Using money intended for bills or essential expenses.
  • Hiding gambling activity from family or friends.
  • Feeling anxious, angry or restless when you cannot gamble.
  • Using gambling to escape financial or emotional problems.
  • Neglecting work, study, sleep or family responsibilities.
  • Repeatedly trying to recover previous losses.
  • Feeling unable to stop even after deciding that you should.

If several of these patterns apply to you, consider stopping gambling, reducing access to gambling services and seeking appropriate support.

When Should You Stop Gambling?

The simplest answer is: stop when you reach either the financial limit or time limit you set before starting.

You should also consider stopping immediately if:

  • you are gambling primarily to recover previous losses;
  • your gambling budget is no longer affordable;
  • you are using borrowed funds;
  • you feel unable to control how often you play;
  • gambling is creating secrecy or conflict;
  • gambling is affecting your sleep, work, study or relationships;
  • you are repeatedly exceeding your own limits; or
  • gambling no longer feels like entertainment.

Taking a break, reducing access to gambling services and speaking with someone you trust can be appropriate next steps.
